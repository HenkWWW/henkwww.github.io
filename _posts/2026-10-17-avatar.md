## But...

Besides hacking and tinkering, I really like drawing. I drew the pig myself in Inkscape: it didn't come off the internet and it wasn't generated with AI.

## Why...

Why a pig? Well, like this. Holten, the village where I live, grew big on the pig farmers who settled there. The old coat of arms of Holten carries three boar heads. In the local dialect a piglet is called a *keun*.

![The coat of arms of Holten](/assets/img/blog/avatar/holten-wapen.svg){: width="200"}
*The coat of arms of Holten, with the three boar heads on the right.*

At carnival, Holten is called Keunedarp. And every year there are the Keunefeesten.

The slaughterhouses and meat processors that used to be here come from the same roots. I started my IT career at the meat processor Enkco. Among other things they ran a vegetarian line, Vivera, which later went independent. Today I've been working there for 30 years.

Holten has no meat industry left today.

Salland and the Achterhoek are full of legends. One of them is the Low Saxon story of *Derk met den bear*. A lot of traditions in the region trace back to it, such as the notorious *slepp'n* in Markelo.

The short version of the story goes like this.

Derk, a farmer, comes into his barn one day and finds all his cattle dead. He got so angry at what God had done to him that he grabbed his air rifle and, cursing and raving, fired into the sky at God. As punishment, Derk had to hunt a pig for all eternity in the afterlife.

On Christmas Eve people say you should bring everything of value inside. Because Derk met den bear comes past like a storm and shoots at anything that isn't nailed down.

So the little pig stands for my roots. From a small village in Salland you don't always count. Research shows that people with a Twente accent are seen as less intelligent, and for various communication roles there are courses to unlearn your accent.

I'm proud to come from Salland. Hence the pig.
