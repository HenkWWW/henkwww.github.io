As a sysadmin I sometimes hear from people that they've been hacked. Afterwards they suddenly want to know how they could have prevented it. Almost always it is the same mistake.

One password for everything. And no two-factor authentication anywhere.

That isn't stupidity, it's convenience. Better security is usually less user-friendly, and that is where it goes wrong.

Below are ten things you can do. If you only do one, do the first one and set up a password manager today. That takes care of most of the risk.

## 10 security tips for your accounts

### 1. Always use 2FA or MFA

Especially on your email accounts. The worst part of being hacked is losing access to your own email. Use an app or a passkey instead of SMS, because SMS can be intercepted. Keep your recovery codes somewhere you can find them. MFA is a hassle. Hackers think so too.

### 2. Your phone is a computer as well

Only install the apps you actually need, from the official app store, and check which permissions they ask for.

### 3. Always update

Better still: turn automatic updates on, then you don't have to think about it. Hackers use the newest vulnerabilities, and with AI that moves very fast.

### 4. Use a passphrase

At least three or four random words. Length helps more than symbols: `bike-lamp-train-bread` is much harder to crack than `Bloempot123!`. Hackers work from lists with billions of passwords from earlier data breaches.

### 5. Don't use the same password everywhere

Say a webshop gets hacked and `pietje@hotmail.com` with the password `Bloempot123!` ends up on a list. What do you think the next step is? Check on [haveibeenpwned.com](https://haveibeenpwned.com/) whether your address shows up in a breach, and change that password straight away.

### 6. Use a password manager

"Easy for you to say, I'll never remember that." That is what a password manager is for, such as Bitwarden or 1Password. It generates strong passwords and you only have to remember one. So not a Word document. I see those.

### 7. Don't use outdated software

Windows 10 has had no updates since October 2025, and an iPhone that no longer receives updates has the same problem. Old and unpatched is wide open.

### 8. Make a backup your computer can't reach

An external drive you disconnect after copying, or a cloud service. And test whether you can actually restore it. With ransomware, this is the difference between a bad week and losing everything.

### 9. Hacked? Get it off the internet

Network cable out, wifi off. Then change your passwords from a device that is definitely clean, starting with your email. If bank details are involved, call your bank immediately. And call someone who knows about this.

### 10. Never click a link in an email or text message

Not even if it looks genuine. Go to your bank's website yourself. A few years ago a spelling mistake gave phishing away; with AI that signal is gone. Look at the real domain behind the sender, not the name shown. Messages like "Someone in Russia is trying to log in, was that you?" can be fake too. And watch the search results: search for "deepseek" and the top results can be ads or fake sites. Type the address yourself or use a bookmark.

And if you're thinking this is a lot: start with tip 1 and tip 6. That's half an hour of work together, and it takes away most of it.
